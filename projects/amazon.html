Delivery sequencing and route feasibility.

Can a feasible route sequence improve on a simple routing baseline without worsening delivery-window compliance?

Independent public-data study · Planned.

Company context

Computed delivery routes can miss driver knowledge about stop grouping and navigation. Efficient sequencing must account for operational constraints and driver patterns.

Data and scope

9,184 real anonymized historical routes operated in 2018: 6,112 training and 3,072 evaluation routes; five US metro areas. Begin with a manageable subset.

Proposed analysis

SQL route/stop/package/travel-time tables. Nearest-neighbor baseline versus constrained local search with service times and time windows. Compare estimated travel time, violations and official sequence score where supported. Separate training/evaluation routes.

How I will test route feasibility

Keep training and evaluation routes separate. Compare a simple nearest-neighbor route with constrained local search. Check stop coverage, service time and delivery windows as well as estimated travel time. Where supported, report the official sequence score separately from feasibility.

The transportation handoff

A route comparison showing the time-versus-feasibility tradeoff and a route-level exception summary. Recommendations remain offline findings because driver knowledge, current traffic and actual implementation are outside the dataset.

Planned deliverables

Route sequence comparison, feasibility checks, route dashboard and transportation recommendation.

Status & limitations

Extraction and analysis have not started. Historical anonymized routes and travel matrices support offline evaluation, not current street routing or realized delivery savings. Driver behavior may reflect unobserved constraints. CC BY-NC 4.0 licensing and attribution apply.
