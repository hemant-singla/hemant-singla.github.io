NIKE, Inc. · Apparel and footwear

Inventory productivity and working capital.

How did inventory productivity change, and how much cash could an inventory-days reduction release?

Independent public-data study · Planned.

Company context

NIKE reported inventories up 44% year over year in Q1 FY2023, including elevated in-transit stock amid supply-chain volatility. NIKE described using direct-channel markdowns and wholesale actions to clear excess inventory. Those responses motivate a study of the inventory-versus-margin tradeoff.

Data and scope

FY2022-2026 annual financial statements plus FY2021 opening balances and selected quarterly figures. Use fiscal periods, not calendar years.

Proposed analysis

SQL sourced statement table. Average-inventory turnover and days, working-capital cash reconciliation and historical event analysis. Model inventory-days cash-release scenarios; examine markdown and freight tradeoffs.

How I will test the cash scenario

Reconcile statement inputs to source filings, retain fiscal-year definitions and calculate inventory days consistently using average inventory and cost of sales. Show sensitivity to the assumed days reduction. Treat the estimate as cash tied up in stock, not a realized saving or profit increase.

The inventory handoff

A five-year inventory-days view and a cash scenario worksheet. The recommendation will weigh working-capital exposure against markdowns and service implications, with clear limits from consolidated data.

Planned deliverables

Five-year inventory/cash dashboard, reconciled metric definitions, scenario model and operations recommendation.

Status & limitations

Extraction and analysis have not started. Consolidated statements cannot identify excess SKUs or service levels. Cash-release scenarios will be sensitivity estimates, not realized savings or profit increases; five annual observations are insufficient for demand forecasting.
