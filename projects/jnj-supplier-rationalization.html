Johnson & Johnson · Healthcare procurement

Supplier rationalization: fewer suppliers, stronger agreements.

Which of J&J’s ~29,000 suppliers should it keep, consolidate or exit, with a data reason for every exit, and what is that worth?

Independent study. J&J does not publish supplier-level data, so every method runs on 344,504 real public purchase orders (State of California, FY2012–15) and the result is scaled to J&J’s published totals. Not J&J work: nothing was implemented, and the J&J figures are modeled, not realized savings.

Key results

  • −73%supplier records (16,933 → 4,649) with a rule-based reason code for every exit
  • 87%of spend stays with the suppliers kept, and 96.6% of small and diverse supplier spend
  • $366Ma year at J&J scale in the base case for $40M one-time; only $86M of it is measured in the data

Scaled by J&J’s published ~29,000 suppliers and ~$35B addressable spend. Savings: $63M measured price gap + $23M from fewer suppliers + $280M assumed from re-tendering kept leverage suppliers. One-time cost $40M; 3-year NPV $572M at 8%.

How much rests on the assumption

$280M of the $366M base case is the re-tender saving on kept Leverage suppliers, which the data cannot measure. Each 0.1 point of re-tender saving adds about $19M a year, so the claim becomes testable: the $300M goal needs at least 1.15% (0.75–1.54% across the range of measured savings). A first sourcing wave under the MSA’s benchmark clause shows whether that holds before the rest is committed.

Does consolidation add supply risk?

Yes, a little, and it is measured. Moving volume to fewer suppliers raises concentration, so I counted the categories where one supplier holds more than half the active spend, before and after the decisions.

Checking the duplicate matching

70 accepted pairs were checked by hand, using the names, ZIP code, main category and department. Exact name matches were right 20 of 20 times; fuzzy matches (similarity 0.88–0.99) 39 of 50 times. That is about 96% precision overall but 78% in the fuzzy band, with errors like “United Rentals” vs “United Van Rentals”. So exact matches merge automatically and fuzzy matches go to a review queue first. They touch 0.6% of spend.

Recommendation

Cut the tail first: merge duplicate records and close inactive and one-time suppliers (low risk, pays back on process savings alone). Then consolidate fragmented categories onto preferred suppliers and re-tender Leverage categories under volume-tiered MSAs. Never cut a strategic or sole-source supplier; put them on partnership MOUs and add second sources for bottleneck items. Approve the re-tender only once the first MSAs show the 1.15% saving the base case needs.

The finding that changed the plan

My first top-down estimate was $385M a year. The price-gap test on 127 goods items bought from both kept and exiting suppliers showed exiting suppliers charged 9.8% more in Leverage and 5.2% more in Non-critical categories, but were cheaper in Bottleneck (−10%) and Strategic (−7%) ones. So price savings only exist where the market is competitive, only $86M of the base case is measured, and the plan pushes price only in Leverage and Non-critical categories.

Real J&J suppliers

Read from the suppliers’ own SEC 10-K filings. Alkermes is the exclusive maker of RISPERDAL CONSTA; MeiraGTx manufactures J&J’s RPGR gene therapy under a 4+1-year supply agreement; Mersana’s clinical supply deal gives J&J a technology-transfer right. Emergent is the counter-example: J&J ended its COVID-19 vaccine contract in 2022 over manufacturing problems, which is why the rules protect sole-source and bottleneck suppliers.

Method

344,504 raw purchase-order lines cleaned in Python and SQLite, every raw row kept with its outcome and reason code. Spend cube and Pareto / ABC segmentation in SQL window functions; a Kraljic matrix of 409 categories; duplicate suppliers found with TF-IDF character n-grams and cosine similarity (scikit-learn); a weighted scorecard and rules R1–R8 for the keep / consolidate / exit decision; a measured price-gap test and low / base / high savings scenarios. Results go to an Excel workbook with live formulas, a Power BI dashboard, a business case and agreement templates (MOU, MSA, supply agreement, catalog terms, transition notices). Eighteen tests check that strategic and sole-source suppliers are never exited, that every supplier and raw row has a reason code, and that totals reconcile from the raw file to the decisions.

Limitations

Public-sector purchasing is a proxy for J&J’s spend, so category mix and prices differ; J&J figures are scaled, not measured. $280M of the $366M base case is an assumption about re-tender savings. Supplier quality, risk scores and contract terms were not available; the price gap uses goods only. Small/diverse status comes from California certifications. Duplicate precision rests on a 70-pair hand check.
