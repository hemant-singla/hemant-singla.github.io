Johnson & Johnson (Ethicon) · Healthcare / medical devices

Recall traceability and reverse logistics.

Can public product/lot identifiers support reliable recall blocking and reverse-logistics controls?

Independent public-data study · Planned.

Company context

Ethicon suture recalls required affected product/lot identification, inventory quarantine and returns. One notice describes packaging seals affected by a manufacturing-machine issue; a separate FDA record describes component mix-up.

Data and scope

Real company product/lot table in a notice published by HPRA in January 2025, covering manufacturing September-October 2024; separate October 2024 MONOCRYL FDA record. Expand to Ethicon records after entity/event audit.

Proposed analysis

SQL recall-event, product/lot, UDI and distribution tables. Extract actual identifiers, deduplicate events, implement product-plus-lot matching and quarantine rules. Flag inconsistencies rather than silently correcting source records.

How I will test the matching rules

Test affected, unaffected, missing and conflicting product-and-lot combinations. Keep every match traceable to its source notice and separate duplicate records from distinct recall events. Ambiguous identifiers must produce a review exception, not an automatic release decision.

The operations handoff

A recall lookup and exception register, plus a quarantine-to-return process map. Any demonstration inventory is explicitly simulated; actual hospital stock or completed returns will not be implied.

Planned deliverables

Public recall register, affected-product/lot lookup, distribution and traceability dashboard, reverse-logistics process map.

Status & limitations

Extraction and analysis have not started. Hospital stock, receipt dates and returns are unavailable; any demo inventory will be labeled simulated. Conflicting FDA lot strings and HPRA placeholders need source reconciliation before operational use. The study will not establish recovery rates or provide clinical recommendations.
